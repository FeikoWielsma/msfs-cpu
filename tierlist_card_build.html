Reading the matrix

Memory is the story in 2026, and it decides the entry platform. 32 GB of DDR5 is €400 against €240 for DDR4 — so entry stays on AM4 and LGA1700 deliberately. Nobody puts €400 of memory in an €1,100 build. Mid and high move to DDR5 where the platform earns it. Nothing takes 64 GB: that kit now costs more than a 9800X3D.

Radeon is better value at every tier right now. A 9070 XT at €669 is both cheaper and faster than a 5070 Ti at €949 (81 against 70 at 1440p), which is why the XT tops the 1440p and 4K blocks while the Nvidia column spends more for less. The 9070 GRE at €555 only earns a place at 1080p, where nothing above it is worth the money.

8 GB is a 1080p-only compromise, and only on the Nvidia side. A 5060 8 GB at €319 saves €243 over a 5060 Ti 16 GB and the penalty at 1080p is the mildest it gets (+29%, against +59% at 1440p). On AMD it is a trap: the 8 GB 9060 XT costs €341 against €414 for the 16 GB, and that €73 buys +66% at 1080p. Cheapest way into V-Cache at all is a 5500X3D, about €150 — AliExpress only, and unbenchmarked, so it gets no row; a 5600X3D reads 61 and it clocks lower.

Intel's ladder is flat for this game, so don't climb it. A 250K Plus (€219) scores 76 and a 285K (€549) scores 78 — €330 for two index points, which is why the 285K appears nowhere on this card. The Intel column tops out at a 270K Plus and spends the rest on the GPU. AMD's ladder is not flat: a 9800X3D reaches 97.

1080p tops out deliberately low. The GPU field compresses to a 5.5× spread there and the CPU becomes the limit, so the 1080p high build pairs the best CPU with an RX 9070 / 5070 Ti rather than a 9070 XT or a 5080 — a flagship card at 1080p is money that buys almost nothing. At 4K the spread opens to 17× and the same money decides everything, which is why only that block climbs to a 5090.

Measured: CPU index from seven Tom's Hardware, PCGH and ComputerBase test passes; GPU index per resolution from Tom's Hardware. Relative scales, not frame rates. Judgement: which CPU pairs with which resolution — CPU tests run at low resolution to isolate the chip, so there is only one CPU ladder. Not measured: RAM and storage.
